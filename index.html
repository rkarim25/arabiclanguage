    The science (and why the numbers start low)
    Screen-known words are discounted for the ear (L2 listeners routinely miss words they "know" — Goh 2000; Field 2008), again for connected speech (finding word boundaries in a stream is its own skill), and — for passages you don't recite from memory — word-catch converts to comprehension non-linearly (van Zeeland & Schmitt 2013; Nation 2006: below ~90% coverage, understanding collapses). For your salah surahs, which you recite daily, catching the words by ear ≈ understanding them, so that panel tracks word-catch directly. Speaking counts only what you've produced (typed in Arabic, spoken past the mic, drilled in Sentences) at full value; the rest is floored at the bottom of the receptive→productive range (Laufer 1998; Webb 2008) and capped by your logged speaking minutes (DeKeyser: fluency needs output hours). Every discount starts pessimistic and rises only as YOUR results accumulate — and placement tests outweigh everything else, so a few honest tests quickly correct any pessimism. One measured fact worth knowing: at 10 focused min/day the model says you can hold about a third of the Umrah set in speech-ready memory; at 15 you hold all of it — the capacity cliff is real.
